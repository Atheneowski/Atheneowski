# Writing TL;DR Messages for GitHub SLT

## Why this matters
Senior leaders move fast. They don’t have time to read full reports, but they still need the signal that drives decisions. A strong TL;DR is your chance to set the narrative: **what’s happening, why it matters, and what action is needed**.

Think of it like briefing a pilot before takeoff, you have one shot to get them the essentials: runway conditions, destination, fuel status, and the “go/no-go” call.

---

## Step 1: Get the story straight
Start by asking yourself:
- **If SLT remembers only one thing, what should it be?** → That’s your **headline**.  
- **What’s the context?** → One sentence of **background** is enough.  
- **Why now?** → State the **problem or opportunity**.  
- **What do we want from SLT?** → This becomes your **recommendation**.  

---

## Step 2: Use the SLT Prompt
Paste the following into ChatGPT (or use it as a manual checklist):

~~~
**SLT Prompt**

Your Task:
Formulate a TL;DR message to GitHub’s senior leadership team (SLT). Think of it as the executive summary for busy leaders: it must be brief, structured, and actionable. The TL;DR should create clarity, drive alignment, and reflect a collaborative voice that supports GitHub’s business vision and delivers optimal value.

Guidelines:
- Keep it under 150 words (ideally 2–5 sentences).
- Always include a clear headline (one line that sets context).
- Include 3–4 supporting insights, phrased as bullets or compact sentences.
- Tie recommendations to business strategy or OKRs where possible.
- End with a recommendation or clear next step.
- Avoid em-dashes. Keep handles, dates, and other identifiers intact.
- Write it so it can stand alone without requiring the full report.
- Tone: crisp, credible, and memorable, with executive altitude.

Structure to Use in paragraph format:
1. Headline – What’s happening / the key message.
2. Background (1 sentence) – Why this matters.
3. Problem/Opportunity (1 sentence) – The “why” behind the ask.
4. Proposed Solution / Recommendation (1–2 sentences) – The action you want SLT to take.
~~~

---

## Step 3: Shape the narrative
Once you have a draft, test it:
- **Headline:** Could this fit on a Post-it and still make sense?  
- **Supporting insights:** Do they feel like evidence, not noise?  
- **Recommendation:** If SLT read only that line, would they know what to do?  

Remember: you’re not just reporting facts — you’re telling the story of why this matters **now**.

---

## Step 4: Deliver with confidence
- Share it in the official SLT channel (email, Slack, doc).  
- Make sure it can stand alone; don’t assume they’ll open attachments.  
- Keep the full report handy in case they ask for details.  

---

✨ **Pro tip**: Think “executive altitude.” SLT doesn’t want play-by-play — they want the flight path and the decision points.

---

**[Headline: one line summary]**

- Supporting insight #3  

Recommendation: [1–2 sentences on the proposed action or decision for SLT.]
~~~
